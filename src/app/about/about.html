Megan's Story

Born Megan Jovon Ruth Pete in San Antonio, Texas, and raised in the South Park neighborhood of Houston, Megan's connection to rap began at an early age. She was profoundly influenced by her mother, Holly Thomas, a rapper who performed under the name "Holly-Wood" and frequently brought a young Megan along to recording sessions. Megan began crafting her own rhymes at age 14, but at her mother's request, she delayed launching her career until she turned 21 to ensure her perspective and artistry had fully matured. While balancing her coursework as a student at Prairie View A&M University, she began uploading fiery, confident freestyling videos to Instagram; one clip of her out-rapping male opponents in a local "cypher" went massively viral, instantly generating a dedicated fanbase she affectionately dubbed her "Hotties".

Megan's mainstream breakthrough accelerated rapidly with early projects like Tina Snow and Fever, culminating in an explosive ascent to international stardom. She secured her first Billboard Hot 100 number-one single with the "Savage (Remix)" featuring Houston icon Beyoncé, followed shortly by her historic, chart-topping collaboration with Cardi B on the hit single "WAP". These milestones propelled her to the 63rd Annual Grammy Awards, where she made history as the first female rapper to win Best New Artist since Lauryn Hill in 1999. Even as her musical career dominated global airwaves, Megan remained deeply committed to her personal development and her roots, ultimately graduating from Texas Southern University with a Bachelor of Science in health administration in late 2021.

Despite her meteoric professional success, Megan's life has been profoundly tested by severe personal grief, intense industry legal battles, and trauma. In March 2019, she suffered back-to-back losses with the deaths of both her mother and grandmother, followed shortly by a highly publicized and painful July 2020 shooting incident involving rapper Tory Lanez. Navigating a public trial, intense online harassment, and complex contract disputes with her former record label, Megan channeled her raw pain and resilience into deeply personal studio albums like Traumazine and Megan, culminating in her first solo number-one single, "Hiss". Today, she stands as an entirely independent artist, a powerful mental health advocate, and a global business mogul who continues to transform her vulnerabilities into record-breaking success.
